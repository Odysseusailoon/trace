Forkscope: Measuring Where Agent Trajectories Fork

YUXI ZHENG · SEP 2026 · WORKING NOTES · QWEN3-8B & QWEN3.6-35B-A3B · SGLANG, DETERMINISTIC INFERENCE

An LLM agent's behavior is a distribution over trajectories. Any single run is one draw from it. We built a replay instrument that measures, for any decision step, the counterfactual outcome distribution od: what would have happened had the dice landed differently. With it we find single tokens that lock an episode's fate, benchmark questions where the model's majority answer is wrong, forks the model walks into confidently (invisible to entropy monitoring), and a thinking budget that multiplies success 50×. Every number below is measured, and the preregistered parts are marked. The whole study cost about $60 of GPU time.

1 · From logits to a word: temperature and truncation

The scene is our measured fork token. The agent has just written SELECT, and the next word decides the episode:  COUNT leads into the integer-division trap (0% accuracy downstream),  AVG into the correct aggregation (100%). One forward pass scores every candidate; temperature and the truncation rules reshape those scores into the distribution the die is actually thrown on. Drag the sliders.

2 · What we measure: the outcome distribution ot

"Was this token important?" is not a measurable question. This one is: at position t, given the prefix generated so far, where will the final answer land — correct, one of the named wrong clusters, no answer? That conditional distribution is ot. Over a whole generation it is a T×C matrix: T positions, each holding one C-way distribution, every endpoint verified by execution.

mass on correct mass on the trap cluster other outcomes

The definition turns two adjectives into shapes. A decision fork is a step change: ot holds a mixture, then snaps to a point and stays there. Drift is movement that never locks. And o0 — the distribution before any token is written — is the task's own success probability, the number a single-sample eval silently replaces with one draw (§8).

3 · How we sample: replay at decision boundaries

Record one episode (T=1.0, tools really execute). At each decision boundary d, rewind to that exact state and sample K=50 full counterfactual continuations — tools re-execute, outcomes are checked against executable gold. The outcome histogram is od; where it collapses is where fate locks.

continuation → correct continuation → trap other / no answer

Replay looks quadratic (K × D full re-prefills). With RadixAttention prefix sharing, the trunk is prefilled once: we measured a 99.2% cache hit over a 6.5M-token replay workload, which makes replay 67–206× cheaper than naive re-prefill. Reruns are bit-identical from derived seeds, so every number on this page can be regenerated.

Two details the fan hides. Redrawing at a cut does not exclude the recorded token — the original path can be re-sampled, so od is the unbiased "replay from here" distribution and the recorded run is one draw from it. And cutting is not editing: we never force a different token; we only choose where to stop trusting the record. Below, the same trajectory cut before and past its fork; where the cut lands decides whether probability mass still flows both ways.

6 · In the wild: BIRD across two model sizes

Same instrument, external benchmark (BIRD mini-dev, 10 questions selected by a frozen two-stage rule: text criteria, then an uncertainty screen keeping questions whose 10-episode answer mode is 4–6 — dispersion, not correctness). 200 episodes per question per arm, four conditions: Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, thinking off and on. First the 8B non-thinking floor — the floor has structure — then what scale and thinking each rescue.

Three findings hide in the floor. First, intent-stall: in the schema-heavy financial DB, up to 56% of episodes hit a column error, write "Let's check the available tables…", then voluntarily stop with budget and turns to spare. Announcing an action and executing it are separable events. Second, same opening, different fates: on b218 the most common correct first SQL is byte-identical to the most common wrong one. The fork sits mid-game, in how results get read, and where a task forks is a property of that task. Third, thinking: with the same weights and seeds, enabling the thinking budget takes b39 from 1/200 correct to 51/200.

7 · Can entropy find the forks? Mostly — and the misses are the story

Cheap idea: only measure where the model hesitates. We tested it on the Forking Fast public release (Llama-3-8B, 100 questions, S=200 ground-truth ot grids): gate positions by 1−pbase, check recall of PELT-detected forks. Amber = entropy-gated recall, olive = random-gate null.

Gating works: about 2× enrichment over random, and recall rises with fork size (0.87 at top-20% for large forks, 0.95 at top-30%). A two-stage instrument, entropy screen first and replay only at gated positions, cuts measurement cost 3–5×. But 13–29% of forks happen where the model is confident. No entropy-based monitor can see them, and the agent data shows why that matters: after the die lands, the trap path in §5 runs at near-zero entropy, confidently doomed.

8 · Fork distributions as a fairer eval

Single-sample evals score one draw from a random variable. Two measured cases show how that lies. On the schema-trap task the modal answer cluster is wrong (46% trap, 18% correct), so greedy and majority-vote evals reward the error. On BIRD b226 the model is a genuine coin flip (o₀ = 0.45).

correct mass modal wrong cluster other outcomes

The distributional alternative is a brittleness profile: success probability o₀ with a confidence interval, the step where fate locks, the self-repair rate, and the coverage of correct strategies. It costs more samples than pass@1, but the same rollouts also provide the attribution in sections 3–6, and prefix caching pays most of the bill. On tasks where the mode is wrong, any eval that trusts the mode rewards the trap. Self-consistency included.

9 · The statistics under the hood

Three pieces keep every picture above honest: how branch samples become legal i.i.d. draws, how smoothing borrows strength without erasing the very forks it hunts, and how to know when an estimate has hit the floor that sampling noise sets.

Mixture draws. At one cut the raw data is stratified: branches with weights, S continuations each. Everything we run downstream — 1/√S convergence checks, cross-run anchoring — assumes i.i.d. draws from one multinomial, and stratified samples are not that. So we mix: draw a branch by its weight, take one unused continuation from it, repeat S times. The result is conditionally i.i.d. from Multinomial(ot), and the standard machinery becomes legal. It is the census trick: stratified sampling, then population-weighted mixing before you quote an average.

The segmented smoother (M5a). Neighboring positions carry nearly the same model state, so borrowing their samples cuts noise — but a symmetric kernel would smear the one thing we are hunting, the step change. M5a segments first (PELT changepoint detection), then smooths within segments, with the kernel hard-truncated at every boundary: segment A never leaks into segment B. Validation: 22/22 synthetic recoveries, TVD estimation error down 36%, effective sample size 3.8× — and on the drift case of §4 it correctly refuses to call any of the 11 wobbles a fork.

Leave-replicate-out, and the noise floor. Is S=20 enough, and is the smoother worth improving? Split the pool you already have: 20 draws in, the rest as pseudo-ground-truth, rotate, and the average TV gap is the typical S=20 error — no new GPU time. Then score an oracle that knows the true ot under the same protocol: its TV is the floor that multinomial noise imposes. An estimator sitting on the floor cannot be improved by modeling, only by more samples. Replicate-TVD shrinks as 1/√S, measured slope −0.493 against the theoretical −0.5.

The chain matters more than the parts: the definition (§2) says what is being estimated, mixing makes the tests legal, break-aware smoothing pulls signal out of S=20 noise, and the floor says when to stop tuning the model and start buying samples. Each piece is standard; the work is the assembly on a new object.
